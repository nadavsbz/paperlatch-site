Page History & Audit Log for Confluence · Last updated 6 October 2026

The short version. The app runs on Atlassian Forge and stores its data in Atlassian's own app storage, inside your Atlassian cloud site. We operate no server, so no data about you or your users is transmitted to us or to any third party.

What the app stores

For each page, attachment and space event that Confluence emits after the app is installed, it stores one record:

DataWhy
The event type — created, updated, moved, trashed, deleted, and so onTo record what happened
The date and time of the event, in UTCTo record when
The Atlassian account ID of the person who caused itTo record who
The page or attachment ID, and the version number at that momentTo tie the event to a specific revision
The page or attachment title, and the space IDSo a record stays readable after the page is gone
For attachments, the file size and media typeTo describe the file without storing it
A gap marker when an event could not be recordedSo the trail can state its own completeness
A purge marker each time retention deletes old eventsSo evidence is never removed silently

The app does not store the content of your pages. It records that an event happened and what revision it happened to — never the text, the body, or the file. It stores no email addresses, no IP addresses and no usage analytics.

One difference from our other app that is worth stating rather than burying: this app does store page titles. It has to, because the purpose of the record is to remain meaningful after a page has been deleted, and at that point Confluence can no longer tell anyone what the page was called. If your page titles are themselves sensitive, that is a reason to weigh this app carefully.

What the app reads but does not store

When a report is opened, the app asks Confluence whether the person viewing it is allowed to see a given page, and it resolves account IDs to public display names so the report is readable. Both are read at that moment and are not written to storage.

Where the data lives

In Atlassian Forge app storage, within your own Atlassian cloud site and subject to the data residency that applies to that site. The data never leaves Atlassian's infrastructure. We have no facility to read it, export it, or connect to it.

Permissions the app requests

  • read:page:confluence — to receive page events
  • read:attachment:confluence — to receive attachment events
  • read:space:confluence — to receive space events
  • read:confluence-content.summary — required by Atlassian to deliver content events to a Forge app
  • read:content.permission:confluence — to ask Confluence whether you are allowed to see a page before the app shows you anything about it
  • read:user:confluence and read:confluence-user — to turn an account ID into a display name in the report
  • storage:app — to store the records described above
  • write:confluence-content — requested and never used. See below.

The write scope, explained rather than hidden

Atlassian requires write:confluence-content in order to deliver page “trashed” and “deleted” events to a Forge app. It is a condition for receiving the notification, not for performing an action, and Atlassian's own events reference notes that the requirement “may be removed in the future”.

The app issues no write request to Confluence of any kind. It never creates, edits, moves, restricts or deletes a page, an attachment or a space. Without the scope it would stop recording the moment a page was deleted, which is the one failure an audit log must not have, so we ask for it and tell you why.

Retention and deletion

Event records are kept for a period a site administrator chooses, 24 months by default. A daily job deletes events older than that. Each run writes a record of what it removed and over what window, so evidence is never deleted without a trace — and if a run fails part way, the two records disagree and the report says so.

Changing the retention period is itself recorded, so a shortened window is visible to whoever reads the trail next.

Gap markers and the per-page summary are never purged. A page whose events have aged out is still reported as a page that existed, which is a different statement from “this page has no history”, and only one of them is true.

When the app is uninstalled, Atlassian retains the app's storage for 28 days before it stops being available to the app, and within 21 days of uninstalling a site administrator can ask Atlassian Support to recover it. Reinstalling the app does not restore data from a previous installation. We cannot shorten or extend that window: it is Atlassian's retention schedule for Forge storage, not ours.

Your users' rights

The stored personal data is an Atlassian account ID and a timestamp, alongside the title of the page acted on. As the operator of your Confluence site you are the controller of that data. Shortening the retention period removes older records across the board; beyond that, there is no way to erase one person's events without uninstalling the app, and we cannot act on such a request on your behalf, because we have no access to the data.

We would rather say that plainly than imply a capability that does not exist.

The Marketplace listing page

The app's listing page on the Atlassian Marketplace is operated by Atlassian, not by us. Atlassian permits partners to attach a Google Analytics measurement ID to their listing page, and we do so in order to see how many people view the listing. That measurement applies only to the listing page on Atlassian's site. There is no analytics of any kind inside the app.

Changes

If this policy changes in a way that affects what is stored or who can reach it, the change will be described here with a new date above, and material changes will be noted in the app's release notes on the Marketplace.
