Page History & Audit Log for Confluence — what it records, how to read it, and what it will not tell you.

Getting started

There is nothing to configure. The moment the app is installed it begins recording the page, attachment and space events Confluence emits. Open Page History & Evidence from the Apps menu to see recent activity across the site, or from a space to work within it.

It records from installation onward. A page that existed before and has not been touched since will have no entries. That is not a fault, and the app says so rather than showing you an empty timeline as though nothing had happened.

Reading a page's history

Enter a page ID, or use the Trail link beside any entry in recent activity. The timeline runs oldest first, because that is the order an auditor reads it in, and each row carries the event, the version number at that moment, and who caused it.

Events recorded: created, updated, moved between spaces, copied, archived, unarchived, trashed, restored, deleted, and permission changes — for pages, and the equivalent for attachments. Page views and likes are deliberately not recorded: enormous volume, no evidentiary value.

State on a date

Pick a date and the app replays the events up to that moment to report what the page's version and status were. It gives one of three answers, and the distinction matters:

  • the state, when the trail covers that date;
  • the trail had not started, when the date is before the app was installed;
  • this cannot be answered reliably, when part of the stored trail could not be read.

The third is rare. It exists because reporting a confident answer from an incomplete trail is worse than reporting none.

The evidence pack

Download evidence pack (CSV) exports the timeline. The file carries its own caveats in rows at the bottom — whether the trail was complete, when tracking began, and whether the space was deleted — so the caveats travel with the file rather than staying behind on the screen it came from.

Each row carries both the display name and the Atlassian account ID. The name is the convenience; the ID is the identifier, and a file carrying only a display name cannot be checked back against the site later.

Deleted pages

A page that is trashed or deleted keeps its record. Two limits apply:

  • A page deleted together with its space produces no page-level event: Confluence does not emit cascading deletion events. The app reports it from the space record and labels it as an inference, not as a recorded event.
  • Once a page is gone, Confluence can no longer say who was allowed to read it. The app will not hand its history to someone whose permission it cannot verify, so the report shows this page has history you cannot see rather than pretending there is none. If this blocks a legitimate investigation for you, write to us — it is a deliberate choice and we would like to know how it lands in practice.

Gaps, and why the app advertises its own failures

The panel at the top of every screen reports whether the trail is whole. If the app could not record an event — a failed write, an unreadable payload, an event with no page attached — it stores a gap marker and shows it there, grouped by cause.

A log that loses an event in silence is worse than no log, because it lets someone sign a statement that nothing happened. If you see gaps, the trail is telling you it is incomplete, and that is the moment to tell us.

Retention

Event records are kept for 24 months by default. A site administrator can change that from the app's screen. A daily job removes anything older.

Two things are never removed: the gap markers, and the per-page summary. A page whose events have aged out is still reported as a page that existed — which is a different statement from this page has no history.

Shortening retention deletes records permanently, and the change is itself recorded so that whoever reads the trail next can see it was made.

Common questions

Can it show history from before I installed it? No. Those events were never sent to the app and no API returns them afterwards.

Can it tell me why something was deleted? No. Confluence does not collect a reason, so no app can report one.

Does it store my page content? No. It records that an event happened and to which revision — never the text or the file. It does store page titles, because a record that cannot name the page is of no use once the page is gone.

Why does it ask for a write permission? Atlassian requires write:confluence-content in order to deliver deletion events to a Forge app. The app issues no write request of any kind. The reasoning is set out in full on the app page and in the privacy policy.

Can other people see history for pages they cannot read? No. Before any page's history is shown, the app asks Confluence whether that specific person is allowed to read that specific page. Where the answer cannot be determined, it refuses.
