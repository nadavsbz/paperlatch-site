Security Policy

Paperlatch · Last updated 29 September 2026

The short version. Our apps run entirely on Atlassian Forge, inside the customer's own Atlassian cloud site. We operate no server, hold no customer data, and make no outbound network calls. That removes most of what a security policy usually has to describe — and it is also why the parts below are short.

Reporting a vulnerability

Email support@paperlatch.com with the word security in the subject line. Please include what you found, how to reproduce it, and the app and version it affects.

  • We acknowledge a security report within two business days.
  • We aim to give an initial assessment, including whether we can reproduce it, within five business days.
  • We will tell you when a fix ships, and we will credit you if you want to be credited.

Please do not test against another customer's Atlassian site, and do not access, modify or retain anyone else's data. Report it to us instead and we will reproduce it ourselves. We will not pursue a researcher who follows this policy in good faith.

Vulnerabilities in the Atlassian platform itself should go to Atlassian rather than to us. If in doubt, send it to us and we will forward it.

What our apps can reach

Each app declares the narrowest set of Forge scopes it needs, and the full list is published on the Permissions tab of its Atlassian Marketplace listing. For Confirm & Chase that is four read scopes plus the app's own storage.

  • No write access to customer content. The apps cannot create, edit or delete pages.
  • No acting on behalf of a user. No ACT_AS_USER.
  • No access to email addresses.
  • No external egress. The apps make no outbound HTTP requests, so there is no third party to trust and no data to encrypt in transit to us.

Where data lives

In Atlassian Forge hosted storage, within the customer's own Atlassian cloud site. Forge hosted storage is data residency enabled: app data is pinned to the same location as the host product and migrates with it. Encryption at rest and in transit, key management, and physical security are Atlassian's, described in the Atlassian Trust Center.

We have no facility to read, export or connect to that data. This is a property of how the apps are built, not a promise about how we behave.

How the apps are built

  • No secrets in the app. The apps hold no API tokens, passwords or shared secrets, and never ask an end user for one.
  • Dependencies. Runtime dependencies are kept to the Forge SDK and its own requirements. Fewer dependencies is the control.
  • Tests before release. Each release runs an automated suite that includes the failure paths — partial reads, refused requests, permission errors — because the rule the code is governed by is that missing, partial or unauthorised data must never be presented as a valid result.
  • Platform updates. Forge apps are updated for every customer at once. A security fix does not wait for anyone to upgrade.

What we do not claim

Paperlatch is a small independent developer. We hold no SOC 2 report and no ISO 27001 certificate, and we have not completed a CAIQ questionnaire. We would rather say that plainly than imply an audit that does not exist. If your procurement process requires one, tell us what you need and we will tell you honestly whether we can meet it.

Incidents

If we become aware of a security incident affecting an app, we will notify Atlassian within the timeframe required by the Atlassian Marketplace Partner Agreement, notify affected customers by email, and publish what happened and what changed on this page.

Changes

If this policy changes materially, the date at the top of this page will change.
