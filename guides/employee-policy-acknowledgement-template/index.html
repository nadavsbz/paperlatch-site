Employee policy acknowledgement: a template, and how to track it in Confluence

Guide · Checked against Atlassian's documentation on 20 October 2026

An acknowledgement is a short statement an employee confirms after reading a policy. On its own it is just a sentence. What makes it useful — to HR, to a manager, or to an auditor — is the record around it.

Below is a statement you can copy, the fields a usable record needs, and three ways to collect it if your policies live in Confluence.

The statement

Keep it short. People confirm what they can read in five seconds.

I confirm that I have read and understood the [policy name], version [version or date], and that I will comply with it. I understand that I can ask [team or role] if anything in it is unclear.

Variations people use:

  • For a new hire: add "as part of my onboarding".
  • For an update: "I have read the changes in version [X]" — so the record shows which version someone agreed to, not just that they once read an earlier one.
  • For a security policy: name the policy owner, so questions go somewhere.

This is a template, not legal advice. If a policy has legal or contractual weight in your jurisdiction, have it reviewed.

What the record needs

A signed sentence is not enough if you cannot later show what was signed. A usable acknowledgement record has five fields:

FieldWhy
WhoA named person, not a shared account
WhatThe policy and the exact version they saw
WhenA timestamp you did not type in by hand
Who should haveThe audience — so you can list who has not acknowledged
HistoryEarlier rounds kept when the policy changes, not overwritten

The fourth field is the one most forms miss. A list of who signed is half the answer; the list you act on is who didn't.

Three ways to collect it in Confluence

1. A table on the page. Ask people to add their name and date to a table at the bottom. Free and immediate. But anyone can edit anyone else's row, the version is not recorded, and nothing tells you who is missing.

2. A form tool, linked from the page. A Microsoft or Google form records names and timestamps reliably. But it lives outside Confluence: when the page changes, the form does not know, and you match the two by hand at audit time.

3. A confirmation app on the page. Confluence has no built-in acknowledgement step, so this means a Marketplace app. The established ones are QC Read and Understood and Comala Read Confirmations; several newer ones exist.
