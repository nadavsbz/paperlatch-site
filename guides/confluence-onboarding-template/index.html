A Confluence onboarding template, and how to know new hires actually read the policies

Guide · Checked against Atlassian's documentation on 21 October 2026

Many teams already run onboarding from Confluence: one space or page tree with everything a new person needs in their first weeks. The structure below works for most teams. The part most of them miss is the last one: knowing that each new hire actually read the policies they were pointed to.

A page structure that works

Keep onboarding in one place, and keep each page short enough to read in one sitting.

PageWhat goes on it
Start hereOne page with the first-week plan and links to everything below
Accounts and accessWhat they will get access to, and who to ask
How we workTeam rituals, where decisions are written down, how to ask for help
Policies to readThe handful of policies every employee must read, one per page
Role pagesWhat is specific to engineering, sales, support and so on
GlossaryThe words and acronyms only your company uses

Confluence has page templates and a Copy option for page trees, so you can keep one master version and copy it for each team.

The checklist

A checklist you can paste into the "Start here" page:

  • Day 1: accounts working, laptop set up, first meeting with the manager
  • Week 1: read the required policies (linked below), meet the team, first small task
  • Week 2: role-specific training, shadow a colleague
  • Month 1: a check-in with the manager on what is still unclear

Use Confluence's task list (type `[]` in the editor) so each item can be ticked and assigned.

The part most onboarding misses: confirming the policies

"Read the information security policy" is usually a line in the checklist. A ticked box tells you the new hire says they are done. It does not tell you:

  • which version of the policy they read,
  • when, in a form an auditor will accept,
  • who has not done it yet, across every new hire this quarter.

If your company is audited (ISO 27001, SOC 2, or a customer's security questionnaire), that third question comes up: show that everyone who joined since the last audit acknowledged the policies. A task list cannot answer it. See policy attestation for ISO 27001 and SOC 2 for what auditors typically ask for.

How to record it

Three ways, from free to automatic:

  1. A table on each policy page with name and date. Free, but anyone can edit it, and nothing tells you who is missing.
  2. A form linked from the checklist. Reliable timestamps, but it does not know which version of the policy the person read.
  3. A confirmation button on the policy page. Confluence has no built-in one, so this means a Marketplace app.

We make one: Confirm & Chase. Put it on each policy page and pick the group that must confirm. For onboarding, that is usually a group your admins already add new hires to. One dashboard then shows, for every policy, who has confirmed and who is outstanding. When a policy changes, a new round starts and earlier confirmations stay on record. It is free for up to 10 users.
